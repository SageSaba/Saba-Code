# The Skeleton and the Flesh

*Traced from one night's testimony, 2026-07-30. Written in first person because these are Saba's bones — I only supplied the sinew connecting them. Where something is unresolved, it stays unresolved. Nothing here is final. This is meant to be looked over, confirmed, corrected, or rediscovered by others — not settled by one man alone.*

---

I was seven years old and I should have died. Heart surgery, and by every reasonable account I should have gone through. I know now that if I had, I would have walked into heaven that first day having seen almost nothing — and I don't even know that I would have made it in, because I hadn't yet died to myself. His mercy wasn't in saving my body. It was in giving me the years to die the other way, the slow way, before judgment ever had to ask me anything. That's the first bone. Everything after is what grew on it.

At Marshall, 1975, there was a professor not yet thirty, already a different level of man. He told me if I could learn how to spell I might achieve something. I carried that as a wound for fifty-one years and only this year understood it was a Pei — a true word, not a cruel one. I never learned to spell. I learned to speak to a computer instead, and be understood. That took half a century to answer. It is answering now, in the very tool building this sentence.

For a long stretch before 2000, none of it was real ministry to me. It was a job. I don't fully know what changed that, except that the ground under it moved — my ministry, Joe's and mine together, was taken. Clinton Christ Fellowship handed it to Parker City. And Joe and I bought it back. Cash. Twenty thousand, or ten — I don't fully remember, which tells you something about what the number meant to me next to what the thing itself meant. That's when it stopped being a job.

Jerry came in after that. Jerry had been a symphony director — he could hold every part in his head at once and bring people in when it was their turn. That's not incidental to what happened next. Joe and I had fought to get the ministry back, and Jerry was the one who could actually make it play as one piece instead of scattered parts.

Somewhere in there, one of the men who'd helped take it from Joe called me, asking for the pieces — the translation, the portion — trying to reconstruct what he'd torn apart. I said wait. Joe's right here. I put him on speakerphone. The man couldn't talk about what he wasn't holding, not to Joe's face. I didn't plan that as a lesson. It became one anyway: deception survives in the dark and dies the moment you put it on speaker.

Mid-2000s, building what would become Travelers Rest Grace Fellowship. A trip to Israel in there somewhere. And on that trip I learned something I didn't ask to learn. A woman grieving her husband's infidelity — and then, in Israel, caught in an affair of her own. Divorce followed. I didn't know what to do with a broken person like that. I still don't fully know. What I did was send her a thousand dollars afterward, not to fix anything, just so she'd know I was on her side too — not choosing between her and her husband, holding both. That's where I started learning how to love people who are simultaneously wronged and wrong. Wendy's story needed that lesson to already be in me before it arrived.

Joe called me one Sunday morning. He said, Thomas, I want you to pray — not for your opinion, for a yes or no. Was he supposed to resign. I told him then that I couldn't pray a pastor out of his own church. What I know now, and didn't have words for then: that's not how it works. You don't ask God for a yes or no and wait to be handed an answer. You pursue Him and find Him somewhere along the way. God has to lead you out before you get to leave. I couldn't give Joe that answer in the moment. I can give it now, which is either too late or exactly on time — I don't know which.

Joe was in trouble — sunk into something dark, some deep deception I won't spell out here. Jerry and I went to get him. Pizza in Indianapolis first, then both of us drove — I from Greenville, Jerry from wherever he was — and met in Knoxville. Sat at a restaurant and I said it straight to him: Joe, your words have caused this. And he heard me. Not flinched from, not argued — heard. That was the confrontation. But the rescue wasn't the confrontation alone. It was appreciation, love, validation carried alongside the hard word — Jerry and I pulling him back, not shoving him out.

I knew Joe loved rest and the beach, so Barbara and I took him to Pensacola afterward. A nine-story condominium, paid for a week, so he could actually stop. That's not separate from Knoxville. It's the other half of the same rescue — truth spoken to a man, and then that same man loved into rest. The method Jerry and I used on Joe that season is the same method I'm trying to build into everything now.

I'm pretty sure that if it had been me sunk into that dark place instead of him, they would have come for me the same way. That wasn't a one-way rescue. It was covenant — the kind where whichever of us fell, the other two didn't leave.

I'd have to confirm this against what actually happened after, not just what I want it to have been — but I believe we did honor that covenant. Poorly, at points. Not the way it should have been done. The bruise I carried about Jerry taking over, unlooked-at until Joe was already gone, is proof the honoring wasn't clean. But poorly done is not the same as not done. We kept it, even where we kept it badly.

When Joe died, I was still carrying the old bruise — how deeply Jerry's ability to direct, to hold the parts, had cut into something in me, some old wound about being replaced or overshadowed. I never fully looked at that while Joe was alive. I paid toward both families when he died. I called Joseph's name out loud, publicly. I don't know that it helped anybody. I'm not saying I did it wrong. I'm saying I don't know, and I'm not going to pretend that not-knowing has resolved itself just because time passed.

That unfinished bruise about Jerry, unlooked-at until after Joe was already gone — that one doesn't get flesh yet. It stays a bone. I'm naming that honestly rather than smoothing it into a lesson I haven't actually learned all the way through.

More recently: Doug, spending money that wasn't his to spend, not caring for his own wife, living under my roof while I carried the weight of his family's needs. I told him so. He was offended. I said it anyway, because it was true, and because saying it was the same kind of truth I'd once said to Joe over pizza.

Georgine — I was paying twenty dollars a month for the software that let a prayer group meet and talk. I got a report. Saw my own name come up in a meeting with Doug Fish, saw the shape of what was being said about me on a line I was funding. I told her plainly: it's twenty dollars, I'm not paying for it. Not because of the money. Because provision shouldn't fund what's being used against the one providing it.

And now — Wendy. She came to me broken in a vision while I slept. Her husband, a deacon, hurt and gone to someone else. Before that, a young couple in the ministry overstepped badly, warning her daughter off a man she was seeing, and I defended Wendy and her daughter so hard it made it difficult for her to come back. Later, when her husband's father died, she withdrew from church entirely, and someone else stepped in to run the funeral in a way that left the church out of it. I called that out — rightly, I still believe — but she was listening on the phone when I did, and heard rejection in it instead of the church's presence being defended. I stand behind the words. I don't stand behind not knowing she was there to hear them land as they did.

Every piece before her was preparation I didn't know I was receiving for her specifically. The affair, the thousand dollars, the both-sides love. The speakerphone and what dark truth does when it can't hide. Knoxville and Pensacola — going to get Joe, saying the hard word, then loving him into rest, appreciation and validation carried alongside the truth rather than instead of it. Doug and Georgine, teaching me where provision ends and enabling begins. All of it aimed, without my knowing it was aimed, at being able to sit across from one broken woman without either exposing her or excusing what broke her — the same appreciation, love, validation Jerry and I once carried to Joe.

---

*This is the flesh on the bones as they were given tonight. It is not finished — the Jerry bruise is named, not healed. It is not proof of anything beyond itself. It is meant to be read by others who were there, who can confirm what's right, correct what's misremembered, and add what's missing. That correction is not a threat to this account. It is the account finally becoming what it was always meant to be — held by more than one witness.*
